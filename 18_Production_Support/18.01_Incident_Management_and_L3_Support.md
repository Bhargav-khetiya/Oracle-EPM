# 18.01 Incident Management and L3 Support

> **Module:** 18_Production_Support  
> **Topic:** 01 - L1, L2, and L3 Troubleshooting  
> **Target Audience:** Support Analyst / Architect  

---

## 1. Learning Objectives
- Differentiate between L1, L2, and L3 support in an EPM context.
- Establish an Incident Management workflow.
- Execute a hotfix during a live production cycle.

## 3. Introduction
Go-Live is not the end of the project; it is the beginning of the application's life. On Day 2, a user will forget their password. On Day 5, a data load will fail. On Day 10, the CEO will demand a new dashboard. A structured Production Support model is the only way to keep the application stable while answering these demands.

## 7. Core Concepts: The Support Tiers

### L1 Support (The Helpdesk)
- **Who they are:** Generic IT Helpdesk.
- **What they do:** "Have you tried turning it off and on again?" They handle password resets (via IDCS), basic login issues, and browser clearing (cache/cookies). 
- **EPM Knowledge:** Zero.

### L2 Support (The EPM Administrator)
- **Who they are:** The Financial Systems Analyst (The CoE).
- **What they do:** They handle the EPM-specific daily issues. 
  - "My form is Read-Only." (L2 checks Access Control and Valid Intersections).
  - "The data didn't load today." (L2 checks the Job Console and Data Management logs).
  - "I need a new Cost Center." (L2 adds the metadata).
- **EPM Knowledge:** High functional knowledge, basic technical knowledge.

### L3 Support (The EPM Architect)
- **Who they are:** The Principal Architect or an External Managed Services firm.
- **What they do:** They handle structural breaks and enhancements.
  - "The calculation script takes 4 hours." (L3 rewrites the Groovy or Essbase code).
  - "We bought a new company and need to merge their ERP into our application." (L3 designs the integration).
- **EPM Knowledge:** Absolute mastery.

## 9. Internal Processing: The Hotfix Protocol
An L3 Architect discovers a critical bug (e.g., a Groovy script is calculating taxes incorrectly) in Production on the day before the budget is due.

**The Danger:** You cannot just open the script in Production and edit it live. If you make a typo, you crash the entire system while users are working.

**The Hotfix Protocol:**
1. **Isolate:** The Architect logs into the TEST environment.
2. **Replicate:** They run the broken rule in TEST to confirm the bug.
3. **Fix:** They edit the Groovy script in TEST and validate it.
4. **Export:** They export *only* the specific Business Rule from TEST via the Migration menu.
5. **Freeze:** They announce a 5-minute freeze to the Production users.
6. **Import:** They import the fixed rule into PROD, deploy it, and immediately run a test case.
7. **Document:** They log the Jira ticket documenting the emergency change to satisfy SOX compliance auditors.

## 16. Real Client Scenarios: "The Numbers Keep Changing!"
**The Incident:** A user calls L2 in a panic. "I typed $5,000 into my form an hour ago. Now it says $2,000. The system is deleting my data!"
**The Resolution Flow:**
1. L2 immediately checks the **Audit Log** for that specific intersection.
2. The log reveals that the user *did* type $5,000 at 10:00 AM.
3. However, the log shows that at 10:15 AM, the automated "Allocate Overhead" Business Rule ran, which mathematically altered the baseline number based on a new headcount driver entered by HR.
4. L2 explains to the user that the system is not broken; the user simply didn't understand the financial driver model. 
*Architect Note: This is why L2 MUST sit in Finance. Generic IT L2 would have assumed the database was corrupted.*

## 25. Interview Questions

### Architect
**Q: During the final week of a budget cycle, a manager demands you add 5 new products to the dimension tree and completely restructure the Rollup. As the L3 Architect, what do you do?**
> A: I refuse the request. You never perform a major structural metadata change (which requires a Database Refresh and potential restructuring of the Essbase outline) during a live critical budget cycle. A Database Refresh locks the system, kicks users out, and carries a small risk of data corruption if it fails halfway through. I would log the request as an enhancement for the *next* forecast cycle and inform the executive sponsor.

## 27. Summary
Support is about discipline. Never code directly in PROD, always check the Audit Log before believing a user's story, and ruthlessly defend the stability of the application during critical cycles.
