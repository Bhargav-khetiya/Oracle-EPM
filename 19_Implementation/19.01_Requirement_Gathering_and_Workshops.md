## 1. Learning Objectives
- Design and execute a successful EPM Requirements Workshop.
- Avoid the "Lift and Shift" trap.
- Translate business jargon into EPM technical architecture.

## 3. Introduction
If you build a flawless Oracle EPBCS application, but it solves the wrong problem, the project is a failure. The most important week of a 6-month implementation is Week 1: The Requirements Workshops. This is where the Architect sits in a room with the CFO and figures out exactly what the company actually does.

## 7. Core Concepts: The "Lift and Shift" Trap

**The Scenario:** A client is moving from 500 massive Excel spreadsheets to Oracle EPM Cloud.
**The Client's Demand:** "Just make EPBCS look exactly like this spreadsheet."
**The Trap (Lift and Shift):** If you build Web Forms that perfectly mimic their broken, chaotic, manual Excel templates, you have just built a $500,000 version of Excel. 

### The Architect's Role
You are not an order-taker. You are a consultant.
- **Challenge the Process:** When they show you a spreadsheet with 50 tabs of manual manual allocations, you don't say, "I will build 50 forms." You say, "Why do you allocate this way? Let me show you how EPBCS can automate this in a single rule using a driver."

## 8. Architecture Overview: The Workshop Agenda

A standard workshop series should be broken down into architectural pillars.

### Day 1: The Dimensionality Workshop
- **Goal:** Define the Chart of Accounts, the Entity hierarchy (Geography vs Cost Center), and the Custom Dimensions (Product, Project, Customer).
- **The Output:** A physical Excel matrix mapping the exact Dense/Sparse configuration of the future Essbase cube.

### Day 2: The Data Integration Workshop
- **Goal:** Figure out where the Actuals live.
- **Questions:** Are you using Oracle Cloud ERP, SAP, or NetSuite? How clean is the data? Do we need to build complex mapping tables in Data Management to translate old SAP codes into new EPBCS codes?

### Day 3: The Calculation / Logic Workshop
- **Goal:** Define the math.
- **Questions:** How do you calculate salaries? How do you allocate IT overhead? How do you calculate depreciation? 
- **The Output:** A list of the 20 Business Rules and Groovy scripts that must be written.

### Day 4: The Reporting & UI Workshop
- **Goal:** Define the Dashboards and Web Forms.
- **Questions:** What does the CEO look at every morning? What does the department manager need to type in? 
- **The Output:** The Navigation Flow design.

## 16. Real Client Scenarios: The "Everything is Important" Client
During the Dimensionality workshop, a Retail client demands that you add `SKU Level` (100,000 members) and `Individual Customer Name` (500,000 members) to the EPBCS Planning cube. 
- **The Client's Argument:** "We need to plan at the lowest possible level to be accurate!"
- **The Architect's Rebuttal:** "If we put 600,000 members into a BSO cube, it will crash immediately. Planning is strategic, not transactional. You do not plan next year's budget for 'John Smith'. You plan for 'Total North America Retail'. We will build the Planning cube at the 'Product Category' level, and we will send the data to a data warehouse if you want to perform transactional variance analysis."

## 25. Interview Questions

### Architect
**Q: During a workshop, the Finance team says they need to maintain 5 different Scenarios (Best Case, Worst Case, Expected, M&A Target 1, M&A Target 2) across 10 years of historical data. What is the architectural implication?**
> A: Maintaining 10 years of history across 5 volatile scenarios inside the primary BSO Planning cube will cause massive database bloat and degrade the performance of the daily data entry for the active budget. I would advise the client to keep only the active 1-2 years of the multiple scenarios in BSO for active modeling. The 10 years of historical data, and any finalized scenarios, should be archived directly into the ASO Reporting cube.

## 27. Summary
The workshop is where you win the project. Guide the client away from their Excel habits, define a strict Dense/Sparse outline on Day 1, and never be afraid to tell the CFO that their requested design will crash the server.
