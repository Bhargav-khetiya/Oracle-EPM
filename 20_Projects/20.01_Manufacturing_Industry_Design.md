## 3. Introduction
Standard corporate accounting (Revenue - OpEx = Net Income) applies to everyone. But if your client is a global Manufacturing company building automobiles, the standard `OEP_FS` Financials module is not enough. You must build a highly customized Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) model that handles raw materials, labor routing, and machine overhead.

## 7. Core Concepts: The Manufacturing Dimensions

When designing the application, you must add specific Custom Dimensions:

1. **`Plant` (or Facility):** A subset of Entity. E.g., `Detroit Assembly`, `Mexico Stamping`.
2. **`Product`:** The finished good. E.g., `Sedan_Model_X`.
3. **`Component` (Raw Material):** E.g., `Steel`, `Rubber`, `Semiconductor`.
4. **`Cost_Pool`:** E.g., `Direct Labor`, `Machine Maintenance`.

## 8. Architecture Overview: The Bill of Materials (BOM) Model

The core of manufacturing planning is the BOM. 
To build 1 `Sedan_Model_X`, you need:
- 1,000 lbs of `Steel`
- 4 units of `Rubber` (Tires)
- 50 hours of `Direct Labor`

### The EPM Execution
You cannot store a dynamic 50-level deep BOM directly in a BSO outline hierarchically; it is too complex. 
**The Solution:** You build a Driver-Based Data Grid (Form).
- **Rows:** Components (`Steel`, `Rubber`)
- **Columns:** The Finished Good (`Sedan_Model_X`)
- **Data Input:** The Engineer inputs the consumption rates (e.g., 1000, 4) into the intersections.

**The Groovy Calculation:**
1. Sales inputs the Forecast: "We will sell 50,000 Sedans."
2. The Groovy script fires.
3. It multiplies 50,000 Sedans * 1,000 lbs of Steel = 50,000,000 lbs of Steel required.
4. It multiplies 50,000,000 lbs * Current Steel Price = Total Raw Material Cost.
5. The final cost is pushed to the Financials cube under `COGS`.

## 16. Real Client Scenarios: The Standard Cost Update
Manufacturing firms use "Standard Costing." They lock the price of Steel at $1.00/lb for the entire year. 
If the real market price of Steel jumps to $1.50/lb, they do not change the Standard Cost in the system mid-year; instead, they calculate a **Purchase Price Variance (PPV)**.

**Architectural Design:**
You must build two Scenarios in EPM: `Standard_Cost` and `Actual_Cost`.
You build a Business Rule (Variance Engine):
`"PPV" = ("Actual_Price" - "Standard_Price") * "Actual_Volume";`
This allows the plant managers to analyze if their lost margin is due to inefficient assembly lines (Usage Variance) or just a bad global commodities market (Price Variance).

## 25. Interview Questions

### Architect
**Q: A manufacturing client has 500,000 unique raw material components (screws, bolts, microchips). They want to track the price of every single component in the Planning cube to calculate the exact BOM cost. What do you recommend?**
> A: Do not put 500,000 raw material SKUs into a BSO Planning cube. It will cause a massive block explosion and render the system unusable. EPM is for strategic financial planning, not transactional BOM explosion. I would recommend they perform the detailed BOM explosion (Screws * Price) in their ERP system (like Oracle Cloud SCM/Manufacturing) or a relational database, and only push the *aggregated* Material Categories (e.g., `Total Hardware`, `Total Electronics`) into EPM to calculate the high-level financial plan and P&L.

## 27. Summary
Manufacturing implementations require you to bridge the gap between supply chain physics and financial accounting. Never load transactional SKU-level data into Essbase; plan at the Category level and leverage driver-based BOMs.
