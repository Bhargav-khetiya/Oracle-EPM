# 23.01 Case Study: The Global Retail Rollout

> **Module:** 23_Case_Studies  
> **Topic:** 01 - Enterprise Retail Architecture  
> **Target Audience:** Principal Architect  

---

## 1. Business Problem
A global retail conglomerate with 5,000 physical stores across 40 countries is currently budgeting in Excel. It takes them 4 months to consolidate the budget. By the time it is finished, it is completely outdated. They have purchased Oracle EPBCS to modernize.

## 2. Current State (The Nightmare)
- Each of the 5,000 Store Managers receives a unique Excel file.
- The manager budgets Revenue per day, by Product SKU (10,000 SKUs).
- They email the Excel file to Regional Managers.
- Regional Managers use copy/paste to combine 500 Excel files. Excel crashes daily.

## 3. Future State Design (The EPM Architecture)

### The Dimensionality Trap
The client explicitly demands that the EPBCS Planning cube must contain `Store` (5,000 members), `Day` (365 members), and `SKU` (10,000 members).

**The Architect's Intervention:**
If you multiply 5,000 * 365 * 10,000, you get 18.2 Billion intersections *before* you even add Account, Scenario, and Version. If you build this in BSO, the system will not just be slow; it will physically refuse to start. 

### The Solution: Strategic Decoupling
The architect must convince the business to separate *Strategic Planning* from *Transactional Execution*.

1. **The BSO Planning Cube (Strategic):**
   - We elevate the dimensions. 
   - `Entity:` We plan at the `Region` level, not the `Store` level (50 members).
   - `Product:` We plan at the `Category` level (e.g., Menswear, Footwear), not the SKU level (100 members).
   - `Period:` We plan by `Month`, not by Day (12 members).
   - **Result:** The BSO cube is incredibly fast. The Regional Managers can model scenarios (e.g., "What if Menswear grows by 5% in Europe?") instantly.

2. **The ASO Reporting Cube (Transactional):**
   - We load the massive 10,000 SKUs and 5,000 Stores into the ASO reporting cube. ASO is built to handle millions of members dynamically. We use this cube for variance reporting (Actual vs Budget), not data entry.

3. **The Data Integration (Pushing it Down):**
   - The Regional Manager finishes the high-level EPBCS budget. 
   - We use an Oracle Data Management script to export the EPBCS Budget back to the ERP system. 
   - Inside the ERP, a relational allocation script pushes the high-level budget down to the specific Stores and SKUs based on historical actuals (e.g., if Store 1 usually does 10% of the Region's sales, it gets 10% of the new budget).

## 4. Lessons Learned
- **Never replicate a transactional ERP inside a multi-dimensional EPM engine.** EPM is for high-level, fast-paced financial modeling. 
- **The "Lift and Shift" Excel mentality is your biggest enemy.** Users want Excel in the cloud. You must force them to adopt best-practice driver-based planning at a strategic level.
