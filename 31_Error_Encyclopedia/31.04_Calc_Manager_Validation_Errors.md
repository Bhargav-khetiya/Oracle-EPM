# 30.04 Calc Manager Validation Errors

> **Module:** 30_Error_Encyclopedia  
> **Topic:** 04 - Deciphering Compilation Failures  
> **Target Audience:** Developer / Admin  

---

## Overview
When you click the green checkmark in Calculation Manager, the compiler reads your script. If it violates the physical laws of the Essbase outline, it throws a validation error. These errors are often cryptic.

## The Encyclopedia of Compilation Failure

### ERROR: "A left parenthesis is expected"
- **The Code:** `FIX "FY24"`
- **The Cause:** You forgot the `()` around the FIX statement.
- **Resolution:** Change it to `FIX("FY24")`.

### ERROR: "Member [X] not found in database"
- **The Code:** `"Total Margin" = "Profit" / "Revnue";`
- **The Cause:** You spelled `Revenue` wrong, or you used the Alias (`Total Margin`) instead of the physical Member Name (`Acct_999`).
- **Resolution:** Check the Dimension Editor. Copy/paste the exact Member Name into the script.

### ERROR: "Argument [1] cannot be a member set"
- **The Code:** `@SUM(@CHILDREN("Total Geography"))`
- **The Cause:** A function that expects a specific member or range was fed a dynamic list (a set). 
- **Resolution:** Usually, you must enclose dynamic lists in another function, or use cross-dimensional syntax correctly. For `@SUM`, you must ensure it evaluates across a valid range. 

### ERROR: "Invalid cross dimensional reference"
- **The Code:** `"Revenue"->"FY24"->"Total Company"`
- **The Cause:** You accidentally listed two members from the SAME dimension in a single cross-dimensional pointer (e.g., if you wrote `"Revenue"->"Expenses"`). The `->` operator requires each member to belong to a *different* dimension.
- **Resolution:** Check your dimension tree. Ensure every string inside the `->` block belongs to a distinct, separate dimension.

### ERROR: "Cannot calculate dynamic calc member"
- **The Code:** `"Margin %" = 10;`
- **The Cause:** You put a `Dynamic Calc` member on the left side of an equation. You cannot physically write data to a Dynamic Calc member because it does not exist on the hard drive; it only exists in RAM during a query.
- **Resolution:** If you want to hardcode a value, the member must be set to `Store` in the Dimension Editor. Otherwise, attach a Member Formula to the member in the Dimension Editor instead of writing it in a Business Rule.

### ERROR: "Missing or Invalid ENDFIX"
- **The Cause:** You opened a `FIX("New York")` but forgot to type `ENDFIX` at the bottom of the script. Or, you nested an `IF` statement inside a `FIX`, and forgot the `ENDIF`, causing the compiler to get confused about where the blocks end.
- **Resolution:** Count your `FIX` and `ENDFIX` statements. Count your `IF` and `ENDIF` statements. They must match perfectly.

## 25. Interview Questions

### Developer
**Q: You validate a script, and it says: "Warning: Script evaluates to 0 blocks." Is this an error? Will the script run?**
> A: It is not a hard error; the script will successfully compile and run. However, it will do absolutely nothing. The compiler is warning you that your `FIX` statement is targeting an intersection where no physical blocks exist, OR the FIX statement logic is contradictory (e.g., `FIX("Jan", "Feb")` where a single cell cannot be both Jan and Feb simultaneously). You must fix your logic.
