# SECTION 1: WHY BUSINESS KNOWLEDGE MATTERS

---

## The #1 Mistake New EPM Consultants Make

They learn the technology (Essbase, Calc Scripts, Forms) but don't understand **WHY** the numbers exist.

```
Client:   "We need to set up expense allocations based on headcount."
Bad EPM:  "What is an allocation—
Good EPM: "Sure — do you want the pool allocated by actual headcount 
           or budgeted headcount— Also, should the IT overhead go 
           to all departments or only revenue-generating ones—
```

An EPM consultant who doesn't understand P&L, CapEx, depreciation, and allocations will build a system that is technically correct but **functionally useless**.

# SECTION 2: THE P&L STATEMENT — LINE BY LINE

---

## What Is a P&L—

P&L = **Profit & Loss Statement** (also called **Income Statement**).

It answers: **"Did the company make money or lose money during a period—**

## Hotel Pvt Ltd — Complete P&L (Monthly: January FY26)

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  HOTEL PVT LTD — P&L — January FY26             │

├──────────────────────────────────────────────────┤

└──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Key P&L Concepts

| Concept | Formula | What It Tells You |
|---------|---------|------------------|
| **Revenue** | Room Revenue + F&B + Banquet + Other | Total money earned |
| **COGS** | Direct costs of delivering the service | What it cost to serve guests |
| **Gross Profit** | Revenue - COGS | Money left after direct costs |
| **Gross Margin %** | Gross Profit / Revenue × 100 | Efficiency of service delivery |
| **Operating Expense** | Salaries + Utilities + Marketing + ... | Cost of running the hotel |
| **EBITDA** | Gross Profit - Operating Expenses | Operating profitability (before non-cash) |
| **Depreciation** | Asset Cost / Useful Life / 12 | Non-cash expense for asset wear |
| **EBIT** | EBITDA - Depreciation | True operating income |
| **Interest** | Loan principal × Interest Rate / 12 | Cost of borrowing money |
| **PBT** | EBIT - Interest | Profit before government takes its share |
| **Tax** | PBT × Tax Rate | Government's share |
| **Net Income** | PBT - Tax | What's left for the owners |

# SECTION 3: THE BALANCE SHEET

---

## What Is a Balance Sheet—

The P&L shows **flow** (what happened during January).  
The Balance Sheet shows **position** (what the company OWNS and OWES at January 31).

```
BALANCE SHEET — Hotel Pvt Ltd — As of Jan 31, FY26

ASSETS (What we OWN)                LIABILITIES (What we OWE)
├── Current Assets                  ├── Current Liabilities
│   ├── Cash         ₹45,00,000   │   ├── Accounts Payable  ₹18,00,000
│   ├── Receivables  ₹32,00,000   │   ├── Salaries Payable  ₹12,00,000
│   └── Inventory     ₹5,00,000   │   └── Tax Payable       ₹10,97,325
│                                   │
├── Fixed Assets                    ├── Long-Term Liabilities
│   ├── Building    ₹15,00,00,000 │   └── Bank Loan      ₹8,00,00,000
│   ├── FF&E         ₹1,20,00,000 │
│   └── Less: Acc Dep (₹2,50,00,000)│
│   Net Fixed       ₹13,70,00,000 │ EQUITY (Owners' Money)
│                                   │   ├── Share Capital  ₹5,00,00,000
│                                   │   └── Retained Earn  ₹1,11,02,675
│                                   │
TOTAL ASSETS      ₹14,52,00,000   TOTAL L + E       ₹14,52,00,000
                                    ↑ MUST BALANCE ↑
```

### EPM Impact

In EPBCS, the Balance Sheet requires **Time Balance = Balance** on Account members (unlike P&L which uses Time Balance = Flow).

```
P&L Account "Revenue":   Time Balance = Flow
  → Jan=₹1Cr, Feb=₹1.2Cr → Q1 = ₹1Cr + ₹1.2Cr + ... = SUM

BS Account "Cash":        Time Balance = Balance  
  → Jan=₹45L, Feb=₹52L → Q1 = ₹52L (LAST value, not sum!)
  → You don't ADD up cash across months — you take the latest
```

# SECTION 5: BUDGETING — THE ANNUAL PLAN

---

## What Is a Budget—

A Budget is a **detailed financial plan** for the upcoming year. Created once (usually in Q3/Q4).

```
Budget Timeline for Hotel Pvt Ltd:

Jul FY25:   CFO sends budget guidelines
             "Target: 10% revenue growth, 5% cost reduction"
             
Aug FY25:   Each hotel manager enters their Budget for FY26
             Hotel India: ₹26 Cr Revenue, ₹18 Cr Expenses
             Hotel London: £5.2M Revenue, £3.8M Expenses
             
Sep FY25:   Finance reviews, sends back for revisions
             "Hotel India — your marketing expense is too high. Revise."
             
Oct FY25:   Final budget approved by Board
             Budget is "locked" — Version changes from "Working" to "Final"
             
Jan FY26:   Fiscal year starts — Actuals begin flowing in
             Compare: Budget vs Actual every month
```

### EPM Mapping

```
Scenario Dimension:
  └── Budget      ← The approved plan
  └── Actual      ← Real numbers from ERP/accounting
  └── Forecast    ← Updated prediction

Version Dimension:
  └── Working     ← Draft — being edited
  └── Final       ← Approved — locked
```

# SECTION 6: FORECASTING — THE REALITY CHECK

---

## What Is a Forecast—

A Forecast is an **updated prediction** that blends Actuals-to-date with future estimates.

```
Example: "6+6 Forecast" created in July FY26

              Jan   Feb   Mar   Apr   May   Jun   Jul   Aug   Sep   Oct   Nov   Dec
Budget:       1.5   1.6   1.8   2.0   2.1   2.2   2.3   2.4   2.5   2.3   2.0   1.8  = ₹24.5Cr
Actual:       1.4   1.5   1.7   1.9   2.0   2.1   —     —     —     —     —     —    
Forecast:     1.4   1.5   1.7   1.9   2.0   2.1   2.2   2.3   2.4   2.2   1.9   1.7  = ₹23.3Cr
              ↑ ACTUAL (locked) ↑                    ↑ FORECAST (new estimates) ↑

→ Budget said ₹24.5Cr. Forecast now says ₹23.3Cr. 
→ Company is ₹1.2Cr BEHIND plan. Management takes action.
```

### Forecast Naming Convention

| Name | Months Actual | Months Forecast | Created When |
|------|--------------|----------------|-------------|
| **1+11** | Jan actual | Feb-Dec forecast | End of January |
| **3+9** | Jan-Mar actual | Apr-Dec forecast | End of March |
| **6+6** | Jan-Jun actual | Jul-Dec forecast | End of June |
| **9+3** | Jan-Sep actual | Oct-Dec forecast | End of September |
| **12+0** | Jan-Dec actual | None | Year-end close |

# SECTION 7: ROLLING FORECAST

---

## The Problem with Standard Forecasting

Standard forecasts end at December. On November 30, you have only 1 month of visibility ahead.

## Rolling Forecast = Always 12-18 Months Ahead

```
Standard Forecast:
  Jan ─── Feb ─── ... ─── Nov ─── Dec ← CLIFF! Zero visibility into FY27

Rolling Forecast (12 months):
  At any point, you can always see 12 months ahead:
  
  Jan FY26: You see Jan FY26 → Dec FY26 (12 months)
  Apr FY26: You see Apr FY26 → Mar FY27 (12 months)  ← crosses fiscal year!
  Oct FY26: You see Oct FY26 → Sep FY27 (12 months)
  
  → You ALWAYS have 12 months of visibility
  → But: requires complex EPBCS configuration (substitution variables, 
         dynamic time periods, automatic data seeding)
```

# SECTION 8: CAPEX VS OPEX

---

| Concept | CapEx | OpEx |
|---------|-------|------|
| **Full Name** | Capital Expenditure | Operational Expenditure |
| **What** | Buying assets (buildings, equipment) | Day-to-day expenses (salaries, rent) |
| **P&L Impact** | NOT immediately expensed — depreciated | Fully expensed in the month incurred |
| **Balance Sheet** | Added as Fixed Asset | Does not appear on BS |
| **Hotel Example** | Buy ₹50L of furniture | Pay ₹12L monthly salaries |
| **EPM Module** | Capital Module (EPBCS) | Financials Module (EPBCS) |

```
CapEx Example:
  Hotel buys kitchen equipment for ₹24,00,000
  Useful life: 8 years
  
  P&L Impact:
    Month 1: Depreciation Expense = ₹24,00,000 / 8 / 12 = ₹25,000/month
    Month 2: ₹25,000
    ...
    Month 96: ₹25,000  (8 years × 12 months = 96 months)
    
  Balance Sheet Impact:
    Month 1: Asset = ₹24,00,000 — Acc Dep = ₹25,000 — Net = ₹23,75,000
    Month 2: Asset = ₹24,00,000 — Acc Dep = ₹50,000 — Net = ₹23,50,000
    ...
```

# SECTION 9: DEPRECIATION AND AMORTIZATION

---

## Depreciation (Tangible Assets)

```
Straight-Line Depreciation:
  Monthly Dep = Asset Cost / Useful Life (years) / 12 months

Hotel Pvt Ltd Assets:
  Building:    ₹15,00,00,000 / 30 years / 12 = ₹4,16,667/month
  FF&E:         ₹1,20,00,000 / 7 years / 12  = ₹1,42,857/month
  Vehicles:       ₹30,00,000 / 5 years / 12  = ₹50,000/month
  Kitchen Eq:     ₹24,00,000 / 8 years / 12  = ₹25,000/month
```

## Amortization (Intangible Assets)

```
Same concept, but for non-physical assets:
  Software License: ₹6,00,000 / 3 years / 12 = ₹16,667/month
  Franchise Fee:   ₹12,00,000 / 10 years / 12 = ₹10,000/month
```

# SECTION 10: ALLOCATIONS

---

## What Is an Allocation—

Distributing a shared cost across multiple departments/entities.

```
Problem:
  Hotel HQ electricity bill = ₹10,00,000/month
  3 departments: HR (20 employees), IT (30 employees), Sales (50 employees)
  
  Who pays— Split by headcount:
  HR:    20/100 × ₹10,00,000 = ₹2,00,000
  IT:    30/100 × ₹10,00,000 = ₹3,00,000
  Sales: 50/100 × ₹10,00,000 = ₹5,00,000
  ─────────────────────────────────
  Total:                        ₹10,00,000 ✓ (must equal pool)
```

### Allocation Methods

| Method | Basis | Example |
|--------|-------|---------|
| **Headcount** | Number of employees | IT overhead by dept headcount |
| **Revenue** | Each entity's revenue share | Group marketing by hotel revenue |
| **Square Footage** | Office/floor space | Rent allocation by area |
| **Equal** | Split equally | Audit fee split across 5 entities |
| **Direct** | Pre-defined percentages | 60% to A, 30% to B, 10% to C |

### Essbase Calc Script for Allocation

```essbase
FIX("Budget", "Working", &CurrYear, @CHILDREN("YearTotal"))
    FIX(@LEVMBRS("Department", 0))
        IF("Headcount" -> "All_Departments" <> 0)
            "IT_Allocation" = "IT_Pool" -> "Corporate" *
                ("Headcount" / "Headcount" -> "All_Departments");
        ENDIF
    ENDFIX
ENDFIX
```

# SECTION 11: INTERCOMPANY AND ELIMINATIONS

---

## What Is Intercompany—

When one entity in the group sells to another entity in the same group.

```
Hotel India charges Hotel London for "Management Services": ₹50,00,000

Hotel India P&L:  +₹50L Revenue (Management Fee Income)
Hotel London P&L: -₹50L Expense (Management Fee Expense)

Group Consolidated P&L: ₹50L - ₹50L = ₹0
→ This is "elimination" — the group didn't actually earn ₹50L from outside
→ It's just money moving from one pocket to another
```

### EPM Configuration

```
Entity Hierarchy:
  Hotel Group (Parent — consolidation level)
  ├── Hotel India (Level 0 — input)
  ├── Hotel London (Level 0 — input)
  ├── Hotel NYC (Level 0 — input)
  └── IC Eliminations (Level 0 — system-generated eliminations)

Account Hierarchy:
  Revenue
  ├── External Revenue
  └── IC Revenue        ← tagged with UDA "IC"

Business Rule:
  "IC Eliminations"."IC Revenue" = -1 × SUM(all entities IC Revenue)
  → This zeroes out intercompany revenue at group level
```

# SECTION 12: CURRENCY TRANSLATION

---

## The Problem

Hotel India reports in INR. Hotel London reports in GBP. The Group reports in INR.

```
Hotel India Revenue:  ₹2,05,00,000 (INR)
Hotel London Revenue: £4,50,000 (GBP)

Group Revenue (in INR) = —
  ₹2,05,00,000 + (£4,50,000 × ₹105/GBP) = ₹2,05,00,000 + ₹4,72,50,000 = ₹6,77,50,000
```

### Rate Types

| Rate Type | Used For | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Average Rate** | P&L items (Revenue, Expense) | Monthly average GBP→INR |
| **End Rate** | Balance Sheet items (Cash, Assets) | Rate on last day of month |
| **Historical Rate** | Equity items | Rate when shares were issued |

### EPM Implementation

```
In EPBCS, currency translation uses:
  1. Currency Dimension: Local, INR, USD, GBP
  2. HSP_Rates entity: stores exchange rates
  3. Business Rule: reads rates, multiplies values
  4. UDA on Account: "Avg_Rate" or "End_Rate" determines which rate to use
```

# SECTION 13: DRIVER-BASED PLANNING

---

## What Is It—

Instead of users typing ₹1,20,00,000 for Room Revenue, they type the **drivers**:

```
Traditional Planning:
  User types: Room Revenue = ₹1,20,00,000
  Problem: Where did this number come from— Nobody knows.

Driver-Based Planning:
  User types:
    Rooms Available    = 200
    Occupancy Rate     = 75%
    Average Room Rate  = ₹8,000
    
  System calculates:
    Rooms Sold     = 200 × 75% = 150
    Room Revenue   = 150 × ₹8,000 = ₹12,00,000/day × 30 = ₹3,60,00,000/month
    
  → Now the CFO can ask: "What if occupancy drops to 60%—
  → Change ONE number → entire P&L recalculates
```

### Driver Tree for Hotel

```
Revenue Driver Tree:

Room Revenue = Rooms Available × Occupancy Rate × Avg Room Rate × Days in Month
F&B Revenue  = Covers per Day × Avg Spend per Cover × Days in Month
Banquet Rev  = Events × Avg Revenue per Event

Expense Driver Tree:

Salary Expense    = Headcount × Avg Salary per Head
Benefits Expense  = Salary Expense × Benefits Rate %
Utilities         = Square Footage × Cost per Sq Ft
Commission        = Revenue × OTA Commission Rate %
```

# SECTION 16: COMMON MISTAKES

---

| Mistake | Why It's Wrong | Correct Approach |
|---------|---------------|-----------------|
| Creating `Budget_FY26` scenario | Bloats cube — every year adds a new member | Use `Budget` + `Year = FY26` |
| Storing Net Income as Store data | Gets stale — should auto-calculate | Use Dynamic Calc with formula |
| Adding Revenue + COGS in same account hierarchy | Makes consolidation wrong | Revenue and COGS should be separate sub-hierarchies |
| Setting Time Balance = Flow on Balance Sheet accounts | Q1 Cash = Jan+Feb+Mar (wrong!) | Use Time Balance = Balance (Q1 = March value) |
| Entering Revenue as negative number | Some systems use -/+ convention | EPM convention: Revenue positive, Expense positive, Net Income = Rev - Exp |
| Allocating by Budget headcount when Actuals are available | Inaccurate allocation | Use Actual headcount for current-year allocations |

# SECTION 17: INTERVIEW QUESTIONS

---

### Beginner

**Q1: What is the difference between Budget and Forecast—**
> Budget is created once a year as a target. Forecast is updated periodically (monthly/quarterly) using actuals-to-date plus revised future estimates.

**Q2: What is a 6+6 Forecast—**
> 6 months of actual data (Jan-Jun) plus 6 months of forecast (Jul-Dec). Created mid-year.

**Q3: What is EBITDA—**
> Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation, and Amortization. Shows operating profitability before non-cash charges and financing costs.

### Intermediate

**Q4: Why is Time Balance important for Account configuration in EPBCS—**
> P&L accounts use Time Balance = Flow (periods SUM up to quarters/year). BS accounts use Time Balance = Balance (parent period shows LAST child value). Setting it wrong gives incorrect quarterly/annual totals.

**Q5: A client wants to expense a ₹50L server purchase entirely in January. What's wrong—**
> Servers are CapEx. They must be capitalized as Fixed Assets on the Balance Sheet and depreciated over their useful life (e.g., 5 years = ₹83,333/month depreciation expense on P&L).

**Q6: How do intercompany eliminations work in EPM—**
> Create an "IC Eliminations" entity. Write a business rule that reverses all intercompany revenue/expense entries at the group level, so consolidated numbers only show external transactions.

### Architect

**Q7: A company has 10 currencies. How do you architect currency translation in EPBCS—**
> Use the HSP_Rates entity to store exchange rates. Tag Account members with UDA ("Avg_Rate" for P&L, "End_Rate" for BS). Write a translation business rule that reads UDA to determine rate type, looks up the rate from HSP_Rates, and multiplies Local currency values. Store results in the reporting currency member.

**Q8: How do you design a Rolling Forecast in EPBCS—**
> Use OOTB Planning Modules with rolling forecast enabled. Configure substitution variables (OEP_CurMnth, OEP_CurYr) that shift monthly. The system auto-seeds future months. For custom: use 24+ months in Period dimension, substitution variables for current period, and a rule that locks actuals and allows input only in forecast periods.

**Q9: When would you use driver-based planning vs direct input—**
> Driver-based when there's a clear mathematical relationship (Revenue = Volume × Price) and when "what-if" analysis is needed. Direct input when the relationship is too complex or subjective (marketing budget), or when data comes from external sources.

## 📝 Summary

| Concept | One-Line Takeaway |
|---------|------------------|
| **P&L** | Revenue - Expenses = Net Income (flow statement) |
| **Balance Sheet** | Assets = Liabilities + Equity (point-in-time) |
| **Cash Flow** | Where cash came from and went (often different from P&L) |
| **Budget** | Annual plan — the target |
| **Forecast** | Updated prediction — the reality |
| **CapEx** | Buy assets → depreciate over time (not immediate P&L hit) |
| **OpEx** | Daily costs → expense immediately |
| **Allocations** | Spread shared costs by driver (headcount, revenue, area) |
| **Intercompany** | Internal transactions → must eliminate at group level |
| **Currency** | Convert using Avg Rate (P&L) or End Rate (BS) |
| **Drivers** | Input assumptions → system calculates financials |
