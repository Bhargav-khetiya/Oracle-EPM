## Overview

Implementing and maintaining Oracle EPM Cloud is a team effort that bridges the gap between Finance and IT. When an enterprise hires a consulting firm (like Big 4, Accenture, or boutiques) or builds an internal Center of Excellence (CoE), the team is divided into highly specialized roles. 

Understanding these roles helps you map your own career trajectory and understand who does what on a massive project.

---

## 1. Oracle EPM Administrator (Production Support)

The Administrator is primarily responsible for the day-to-day health, maintenance, and operational stability of the EPM environment after the project goes live.

### Key Responsibilities:
- **System Maintenance:** Managing the Automated Maintenance Window (AMW) and reviewing monthly Oracle patch notes.
- **Application Management:** Provisioning new users, setting up security roles, and auditing access controls.
- **Metadata Management:** Approving and adding new accounts or cost centers requested by the business.
- **Support:** Acting as the primary point of contact for end-user support (e.g., "I can't see this form!").
- **Automation:** Running daily `epmautomate` scripts to load actuals from ERP.

### Skills Needed:
- Strong organizational skills.
- Understanding of EPM Automate and basic shell/batch scripting.
- Deep knowledge of IDCS (Identity Cloud Service) and EPM security models.

---

## 2. Oracle EPM Developer (Technical Consultant)

The Developer (or Technical Consultant) focuses on the **build and configuration** of the EPM solutions based on designs provided by Architects or Functional Consultants. **This repository is heavily focused on training you for this role.**

### Key Responsibilities:
- **System Configuration:** Building Essbase cubes, creating dimensions, and designing complex web forms.
- **Logic & Calculations:** Developing heavy Business Rules (Calc Scripts) to automate math. (e.g., "Calculate depreciation for all assets based on their lifespan.")
- **Data Integration:** Building pipelines in Data Management/Data Integration to map ERP accounts to EPM accounts.
- **Performance Tuning:** Optimizing rules to ensure they run in seconds, not hours.

### Skills Needed (The Tech Stack):
- **Oracle Essbase Business Rules:** The standard procedural calculation language.
- **Groovy Scripting:** The modern, API-driven scripting language used in Planning.
- **MDX Queries:** Multi-Dimensional eXpressions (like SQL, but for cubes).
- **SQL Queries:** Used when dealing with underlying relational databases in Data Integration.
- Deep understanding of Data Structures (Dense/Sparse, Block Storage).

---

## 3. Oracle EPM Functional Consultant

The Functional Consultant acts as the bridge between the client's business requirements and the technical solution. They speak the language of **finance and accounting**.

### Key Responsibilities:
- **Requirement Gathering:** Leading workshops with the CFO and Controllers. ("Do you plan by month or by quarter— What are your key revenue drivers—)
- **Documentation:** Writing the Functional Design Documents (FDDs).
- **Solution Design:** Deciding *how* to map the business process into EPM. ("We need 3 custom dimensions for this model.")
- **Testing & Training:** Conducting User Acceptance Testing (UAT) and creating training manuals for the finance team.

### Skills Needed:
- Strong understanding of accounting and finance (P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, GAAP/IFRS).
- Excellent communication, presentation, and interviewing skills.
- Deep functional knowledge of EPM modules (e.g., knowing the difference between Workforce and Capital out-of-the-box modules).
- Often holds a CPA, CA, or MBA in Finance.

---

## 4. Oracle EPM Solution Architect

The Architect is a high-level strategic role, usually held by veterans with 10+ years of experience. They are responsible for the end-to-end design, scalability, and integrity of the EPM solution.

### Key Responsibilities:
- **Strategic Advisory:** Advising clients on best practices, module selection (e.g., "You need FCCS, not just Planning"), and long-term platform roadmap.
- **Solution Architecture:** Designing the global dimension framework. If the architect designs the "Entity" dimension poorly on Day 1, the entire project will fail.
- **Integration Design:** Designing how EPM talks to Oracle Fusion, Workday, or SAP.
- **Delivery Ownership:** Ensuring the technical build by the Developers actually solves the Functional Consultant's gathered requirements.

### Skills Needed:
- Mastery of both the Functional and Technical domains (a "Techno-Functional" unicorn).
- Deep understanding of Hybrid BSO, ASO, and OLAP performance tuning.
- Strong executive presence to present to C-level stakeholders.

## 5. How They Work Together (The Workflow)

A typical day on a consulting project looks like this:

1. **Functional Consultant** meets the CFO: *"How do you calculate employee bonuses—*
2. **CFO:** *"It's 10% of their base salary if they've been here over a year, otherwise 5%."*
3. **Functional Consultant** writes the requirement: *"Req 14: Bonus calculation logic based on tenure."*
4. **Architect** designs the solution: *"We need a 'Tenure' driver in the Workforce cube, structured as a Smart List."*
5. **Technical Developer** builds the rule: Writes a **Groovy script** that calculates `Bonus = Base Salary × 0.10` if the Smart List value is `> 1`.
6. **Functional Consultant** tests the rule: Enters a $100k salary in the test environment, verifies the bonus calculates as $10k.
7. **Administrator** moves the rule to Production during the maintenance window.
8. **Client** signs off!

## Real Client Scenario

> **Client:** Global Manufacturing Company implementing EPM Planning.  
> **The Problem:** The client complained that the "system was broken" because they couldn't see their new departments in the planning forms.  
> **The Team Response:** 
> - The **Functional Consultant** confirmed the new departments were valid business units.
> - The **Administrator** added the new departments to the Entity dimension via EDMCS.
> - The **Developer** realized the new departments required a unique allocation rule, and updated the Groovy script.
> - The **Architect** updated the global data map to ensure ERP actuals would flow into these new departments next month.

## Interview Questions

- **Q: What is the difference between a Functional Consultant and a Technical Developer in EPM—**
  - *A: A Functional Consultant focuses on business requirements, accounting processes, and user training. A Technical Developer focuses on writing Calc Scripts, Groovy, building integrations, and optimizing cube performance.*

- **Q: Who is responsible for designing the overall dimension structure of the application—**
  - *A: The EPM Solution Architect. It requires a holistic view of performance, reporting needs, and integration limitations.*
