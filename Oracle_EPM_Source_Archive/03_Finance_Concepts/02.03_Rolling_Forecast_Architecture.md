## WHY — What Real-World Problem Does This Solve?

Traditional budgeting is obsolete. A company spends 3 months in Autumn building a massive `FY26` budget. By March of `FY26`, a new competitor opens across the street, a global pandemic hits, or supply chain costs triple. The budget locked in January is now mathematically useless.

Management needs a fluid, constantly updating financial model that always looks ahead.

If Hotel Pvt Ltd is in October FY26, the traditional forecast ends in December. The CFO has only 2 months of visibility into the future. That is flying blind. They need a **Rolling Forecast** so that in October, they are forecasting out to September FY27.

The architectural challenge? Essbase naturally organizes time into static Year boxes (`FY26`, `FY27`). Building web forms and calculations that dynamically "leap" across the Year boundary every single month without manual developer intervention is one of the hardest design challenges in EPM.

---

## WHAT — Plain English Definition

### Traditional Budget
A static financial plan locked for a 12-month fiscal year (Jan to Dec). It never changes.

### Standard Forecast (Year-to-Go)
A blend of actuals and future predictions that ends at the fiscal year boundary. 
- In March: 3 months Actual, 9 months Forecast (3+9).
- In October: 10 months Actual, 2 months Forecast (10+2). The horizon gets shorter as the year progresses.

### Rolling Forecast
A forecast that always maintains a constant 12-month (or 18-month) horizon into the future, regardless of the fiscal year. 
- In March FY26, you forecast Apr FY26 to Mar FY27. 
- In October FY26, you forecast Nov FY26 to Oct FY27.
- **The horizon never shrinks.**

---

## HOW — The Dimensional Architecture Challenge

The Rolling Forecast physically breaks the standard `Year` / `Period` dimensional grid in Essbase.

### The Essbase Problem
In standard reporting, an intersection is `Year: FY26` -> `Period: Oct`.
If a user is looking at October, they want to see the next 12 months. This requires a web form to show `FY26 (Nov, Dec)` AND `FY27 (Jan, Feb... Oct)` simultaneously in the columns.

If you hardcode these columns in a web form, an EPM Admin must manually edit the form on the 1st of every single month to shift the columns forward. For 50 forms, this is a maintenance nightmare.

### The Architect's Solution: Substitution Variable Matrices

You must decouple the form columns from static years by using a matrix of Substitution Variables.

#### Step 1: Define the Variables
Create 12 pairs of Substitution Variables (one for the Month, one for the Year).
- `&M1`, `&Y1`
- `&M2`, `&Y2`
...
- `&M12`, `&Y12`

#### Step 2: Build the Web Form
Instead of hardcoding "Jan" and "FY26", you build the columns using ONLY the variables:
- **Column 1:** `&M1` / `&Y1`
- **Column 2:** `&M2` / `&Y2`
- **Column 12:** `&M12` / `&Y12`

#### Step 3: The Automation Engine
On the last day of the month, an automated script (EPM Automate or Groovy) calculates the new time horizon and updates all 24 variables in the background.

When the planners log in on the 1st of the month, the web form has magically shifted forward one month, smoothly crossing the FY26/FY27 boundary, without a single line of code changing in the form itself.

## SHOW ME — Hotel Pvt Ltd Rolling Forecast

### The Business Requirement
Hotel Pvt Ltd wants a 12-Month Rolling Forecast for Room Occupancy. It is currently the end of **August FY26**.

### The Variable State (End of August)
The EPM Automation script sets the variables as follows:
- `&M1` = Sep, `&Y1` = FY26
- `&M2` = Oct, `&Y2` = FY26
- `&M3` = Nov, `&Y3` = FY26
- `&M4` = Dec, `&Y4` = FY26
- `&M5` = Jan, `&Y5` = FY27  *(← Notice the Year boundary cross!)*
- `...`
- `&M12` = Aug, `&Y12` = FY27

### The Web Form Rendering
```text
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Hotel India - 12 Month Rolling Room Occupancy Forecast                      │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                 | &M1 &Y1 | &M2 &Y2 | &M3 &Y3 | &M4 &Y4 | &M5 &Y5 |   ...   │
│                 | Sep-26  | Oct-26  | Nov-26  | Dec-26  | Jan-27  |         │
├─────────────────┼─────────┼─────────┼─────────┼─────────┼─────────┼─────────┤
│ Available Rooms |   6000  |   6200  |   6000  |   6200  |   6200  |         │
│ Occupancy %     |    72%  |    78%  |    85%  |    92%  |    65%  |         │
│ Room Revenue    |  ₹1.3Cr |  ₹1.5Cr |  ₹1.8Cr |  ₹2.2Cr |  ₹0.9Cr |         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**What happens on October 1st?**
The script runs. `&M1` becomes Oct-26. `&M2` becomes Nov-26. `&M12` becomes Sep-27. The form dynamically shifts to the right without the IT team lifting a finger.

## MISTAKES — Common Errors & Fixes

| # | Mistake | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---------|---------|-----|
| 1 | Hardcoding Form Columns | Admin spends 2 hours every month updating 50 forms | Redesign all forms to use a standard matrix of substitution variables (`&RF_M1`, etc.). |
| 2 | Forgetting the "Current Year" variable | Calculations copy data to the wrong year | You must maintain a `&CurYr` and `&CurMo` variable alongside the matrix so business rules know what is "Actuals" vs "Forecast". |
| 3 | OOTB Rolling Forecast Limitations | The out-of-the-box (OOTB) Planning module creates a massive sparse dimension for Rolling Periods | The OOTB feature creates members like `OEP_Roll1`, `OEP_Roll2`. This inflates the database size. The Substitution Variable method is much cleaner and faster. |
| 4 | Ignoring Year-to-Date (YTD) Needs | Planners cannot see their full-year bonus targets | A rolling form shows 12 months, but planners still need to see the *Total FY26* picture. Always include a static YTD Actuals column and a `Full Year FY26 Forecast` column at the end of the form. |

## TEST ME — Interview Questions

### 🟢 Beginner
**Q: What is the primary difference between a traditional budget and a rolling forecast?**
> A: A traditional budget is static, spans 12 months of a single fiscal year, and is locked in. A rolling forecast is continuously updated every month and maintains a constant future horizon (e.g., 12 or 18 months), allowing the business to see past the end of the current fiscal year.

### 🟡 Intermediate
**Q: Why is building a Rolling Forecast web form difficult in Essbase?**
> A: Because standard time dimensions are split into `Year` (FY26) and `Period` (Jan-Dec). A rolling forecast naturally crosses the boundary between December and January. Hardcoding columns requires a developer to manually update the form layout every single month to shift the years.

### 🔴 Architect
**Q: A client wants a 15-month rolling forecast. They want a Web Form that dynamically updates every month to show the 15-month horizon. How do you architect the columns of this form so that you do not have to edit the form layout manually every 30 days?**
> A: I would completely decouple the form columns from static dimension members. I would build the form columns entirely out of Substitution Variables. I would define variables for the Period (`&M1` through `&M15`) and corresponding variables for the Year (`&Y1` through `&Y15`) because a 15-month forecast will always cross at least one fiscal year boundary. I would cross-join the Period and Year variables in the form columns. Finally, I would write an EPM Automate or Groovy routine that programmatically updates these 30 variables on the last day of the month, achieving a 100% automated rolling UI.
