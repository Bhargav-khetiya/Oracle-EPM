## 🏢 1. Driver-Based Planning

This is one of the **most important concepts** in modern financial planning.

### What is it?
Instead of manually typing in every single expense or revenue number, you enter a few key inputs (called **Drivers**), and the system automatically calculates the rest.

### Example 1 — Manpower (HR) Planning

**Traditional Planning:**
- "Let's budget ₹10 Crore for salaries this year."

**Driver-Based Planning:**
- **Driver 1:** Current Employees = 100
- **Driver 2:** Average Salary = ₹10 Lakh
- **Driver 3:** Planned New Hires = 20
- **System Calculation:** Total Salary Expense = (100 + 20) × ₹10 Lakh = **₹12 Crore**

> If HR decides to hire 30 people instead, they just change the driver to "30", and the **entire budget automatically recalculates**.

### Example 2 — Revenue Planning (From your Hotel Project)

- **Driver 1:** Number_of_Meals
- **Driver 2:** Selling_Price_Per_Meal
- **Calculation:** Revenue = Number_of_Meals × Selling_Price_Per_Meal

### Common Examples of Drivers:
- Number of employees
- Product price
- Units sold
- Salary per employee
- Rent per square foot
- Tax rate

## 💡 2. Rolling Forecast

### What is a Forecast?
A forecast is a **prediction** of what will happen in the future, based on what has already happened.

### What is a *Rolling* Forecast?
A rolling forecast is a forecast that is **always updated** and always looks ahead by a fixed number of months (usually 12 or 18 months). As one month passes, it "drops off" the forecast and a new month is added to the end.

### Example:

**January:**
- We plan the year. Forecasted Revenue = **₹100 Crore**.

**March Arrives:**
- We now know the *Actual* results for January and February.
- Maybe January and February revenues were higher than expected.
- We update the forecast for the rest of the year.
- New Forecasted Revenue = **₹115 Crore**.

> **It "rolls" forward as new information arrives.** You are never planning based on old, outdated assumptions.

### 💡 Logic & Purpose
Traditional budgets are "static." You create a budget for FY26 in October of FY25. By July FY26, a global event (like a pandemic or supply chain shock) occurs, making the budget 100% useless. A Rolling Forecast dynamically ingests the *Actuals* from the ERP every single month, compares them to the Budget, and forces the business to continuously re-plan the next 12-18 months based on current reality.

### 🌀 Confusion Resolution
> **Misconception:** "We turned on Rolling Forecasts, so we don't need to do an Annual Budget anymore."
> **Reality:** An Annual Budget is still required for statutory commitments, shareholder guidance, and executive compensation targets (bonuses). The Budget is the fixed target you *aim* for; the Rolling Forecast is your current trajectory showing where you will actually *land*.

### 😈 Twisted Interview Question
**Question:** A client wants a Rolling Forecast that looks out exactly 18 months at all times. They tell you to create 18 new "Month" members in the Period dimension (e.g., Month1, Month2... Month18). Is this the correct design?
> **Answer:** No! The Period dimension should ONLY contain standard calendar periods (Jan, Feb, Mar... Q1... YearTotal). Creating rolling generic months destroys Time Series calculations (like YTD). The correct design is to use standard Months and Years, and let the EPBCS Rolling Forecast module dynamically cross the Years and Periods using Substitution Variables to generate the 18-month rolling window on data forms.

## 🛠 3. What-If Analysis

### What is it?
Testing hypothetical business scenarios without affecting the official budget.

### Example:
> The CFO asks: *"What if the government increases the FSSAI License fee by 20%? How will that affect our Net Profit?"*

**How to do it in EPBCS:**
1. Switch to the **"What-If" Version** dimension.
2. Change the driver "FSSAI License Fee" to +20%.
3. The system calculates the new Net Profit.
4. If the CFO likes the plan, they can copy the "What-If" numbers to the "Working" version. If not, they just discard it.

## 💡 4. Implied Sharing (Essbase Concept)

We touched on this in Document 07, but it's important enough to repeat.

### What is it?
**Implied Sharing** is an Essbase behavior where a parent member automatically shares its data value with its only child.

### Example:
```
Revenue (Parent)
└── Product_A_Revenue (Child)
```
If you have only ONE product right now, Essbase says: *"The total Revenue is just the Product A Revenue. I won't calculate it; I will just 'share' the value."*

### Why does this matter?
If you later add a second product, the parent will stop "sharing" and will start "adding" the children together.
```
Revenue (Parent)
├── Product_A_Revenue (Child)
└── Product_B_Revenue (Child)
```

## 📊 5. Substitution Variables

### What are they?
Global variables that act as placeholders. They are defined once at the application level and can be used in forms, business rules, and reports.

### Example:
- Variable: `&CurrentMonth`
- Value: `Jun`

Instead of hardcoding "Jun" in 50 forms, you use `&CurrentMonth`. When July arrives, the administrator changes the value to `Jul` ONE TIME, and all 50 forms automatically update.

## 🏋 6. UDA (User-Defined Attributes)

### What are they?
Custom text tags attached to dimension members, used to easily filter or group members in forms and business rules.

### Example:
- Attach the UDA "FixedCost" to Rent, Insurance, and Salaries.
- Attach the UDA "VariableCost" to Raw Materials and Commission.
- Now, you can easily create a form that says: *"Show me all expenses with the UDA 'FixedCost'."*

## 🎛️ 7. Consolidation Properties

Consolidation is the process of adding numbers up the hierarchy. 

### Key Rule: Source Combination = Load Value
When data is loaded from a source system (like ERP) into EPM, it loads at the **Level 0 (leaf)** members.

- Data is loaded into `E10001` (Hotel) and `E10002` (Manufacturing).
- EPM then **consolidates** (adds up) these values to `Total Entity`.
