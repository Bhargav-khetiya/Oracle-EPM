## 📌 Q1. What is Essbase—
> **Answer:** Essbase (Extended Spreadsheet Database) is Oracle's multidimensional database engine that powers EPM. Unlike relational databases (rows and columns), Essbase stores data in multi-dimensional cubes — making financial calculations and reporting extremely fast.

---

## 📌 Q2. What is a Cube in Essbase—
> **Answer:** A Cube (also called a Database) is a multi-dimensional data structure. Think of it as a 5D+ spreadsheet where each axis is a business dimension (Account, Entity, Period, Year, Scenario). Each cell at the intersection of all dimensions holds a data value.

---

## 📌 Q3. What is the difference between Dense and Sparse dimensions—
> **Answer:**
> - **Dense:** Dimensions where most combinations have data. They form the interior of each data block. (Typically: Account, Period)
> - **Sparse:** Dimensions where many combinations have NO data. Each unique sparse combination with data creates a separate block. (Typically: Entity, Scenario, Version, Year)

**Follow-up:** How do you decide which is which—
> Look at data patterns. If almost every combination has a value → Dense. If data is patchy → Sparse. Rule of thumb: Account and Period are almost always Dense.

---

## 📌 Q4. What is a Data Block—
> **Answer:** A data block is a multi-dimensional array that stores data for one specific combination of all Sparse dimensions. The block's interior is defined by all Dense dimensions crossed together.

> Example: Block for [Entity=E10001, Year=FY26, Scenario=Budget] contains an Account × Period matrix (all Dense dimensions).

---

## 📌 Q5. How does Essbase retrieve a specific value—
> **Answer:** Two-step process:
> 1. **Index Lookup:** Use the sparse dimension members to find the block's location in the index
> 2. **Cell Lookup:** Use the dense dimension members to find the specific cell within that block

---

## 📌 Q6. How do you calculate block size—
> **Answer:** Block Size = Product of all Dense dimension member counts × 8 bytes per cell.
> Example: Account (10 members) × Period (12 members) = 120 cells × 8 bytes = 960 bytes per block.

**Follow-up:** What is the recommended block size—
> Oracle recommends 100KB to 200KB. If blocks are too large, they consume too much memory. If too small, there are too many blocks and the index becomes bloated.

---

## 📌 Q7. What is the difference between BSO and ASO—
> **Answer:**
> | Feature | BSO | ASO |
> |---|---|---|
> | Data Entry | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
> | Complex Calculations | ✅ Yes | ❌ Limited |
> | Aggregation Speed | Moderate | Very Fast |
> | Use Case | Planning & Budgeting | Reporting & Analysis |

**Follow-up:** What is Hybrid BSO—
> The modern default. Combines BSO's write-back and calculation power with ASO-like dynamic aggregation speed.

---

## 📌 Q8. What are the Data Storage types—
> **Answer:**
> - **Store:** Data physically saved, user can enter data
> - **Dynamic Calc:** Calculated on-the-fly, NOT stored (saves space)
> - **Dynamic Calc and Store:** Calculated once, then cached
> - **Never Share:** Unique storage, prevents aggregation (for rates/prices)
> - **Shared:** References another member's data (pointer)
> - **Label Only:** No data — just a grouping header

---

## 📌 Q9. What is Implied Sharing—
> **Answer:** When a parent has exactly one child, Essbase automatically shares the child's value with the parent. No separate storage or calculation is needed. Adding a second child breaks implied sharing.

---

## 📌 Q10. What are Levels and Generations—
> **Answer:**
> - **Generation:** Top-down counting. Root = Gen 1, first children = Gen 2, etc.
> - **Level:** Bottom-up counting. Leaf members = Level 0, one above = Level 1, etc.

---

## 📌 Q11. What are the Consolidation Operators—
> **Answer:** They control how children contribute to their parent:
> - `+` Addition, `-` Subtraction, `*` Multiplication, `/` Division
> - `~` Ignore (don't include), `^` Never consolidate
> - `%` Percentage

---

## 📌 Q12. What are the Time Balance options—
> **Answer:**
> - **Flow:** Sum all periods (Revenue, Expense)
> - **First:** Take first period only (Opening balance)
> - **Balance:** Take last period only (Cash balance)
> - **Average:** Simple average across periods
> - **Fill:** Carry last value forward (Fixed rent)

**Follow-up:** Why can't you use Flow for Cash Balance—
> Because Flow would ADD Jan cash + Feb cash + Mar cash, giving a meaningless total. Cash Balance should show the ending balance (Balance = last month's value).

---

## 📌 Q13. What is the difference between Account Types—
> **Answer:** Account Type determines financial behavior:
> - **Revenue:** Income accounts, Non-Expense variance
> - **Expense:** Cost accounts, Expense variance (negative = favorable)
> - **Asset:** Things owned (Cash, Equipment)
> - **Liability:** Things owed (Loans, Payables)
> - **Equity:** Owner's investment
> - **Saved Assumption:** Rates/drivers (Tax Rate, Growth %)

---

## 📌 Q14. What happens when you change a member from Store to Dynamic Calc—
> **Answer:** The stored data is LOST. The member will now calculate its value from its formula or by aggregating its children. You cannot enter data into a Dynamic Calc member.

### 😈 Twisted Interview Question
**Question:** An admin changes 'Net Income' from `Store` to `Dynamic Calc` in DEV, and the cube restructures fine. However, when they migrate the application to PROD via LCM, the migration fails with an error: "Cannot change data storage for a member that contains data." Why did it work in DEV but fail in PROD—
> **Answer:** In DEV, they likely cleared the data before restructuring, or DEV had no data. Essbase absolutely refuses to convert a `Store` member to `Dynamic Calc` if there is physical data sitting in that intersection! The admin must run a `CLEARDATA "Net Income"` business rule in PROD to wipe the physical blocks *before* running the LCM migration.

---

## 📌 Q15. Why would you use Never Share instead of Store—
> **Answer:** For rates, prices, and percentages that should NOT aggregate. Example: Selling Price = ₹200/meal. Without Never Share, Q1 price = Jan (₹200) + Feb (₹200) + Mar (₹200) = ₹600, which is nonsensical. Never Share prevents this.
